The Problem

The existing recruitment experience did not sufficiently communicate the value of the company to graduate candidates.

Important information was distributed across multiple pages, making it difficult for users to understand the company, available opportunities, and what they could expect from the recruitment process.

This created unnecessary friction during the process of researching and comparing potential employers.

Research Objectives

The research aimed to understand how graduate candidates discover, evaluate, and make decisions about potential employers during the recruitment process.

Objective 01

Understand the key information candidates need when researching a potential employer.

Objective 02

Identify barriers and motivations throughout the graduate recruitment journey.

Research Findings

The research revealed several key patterns in how candidates evaluate recruitment information and form their first impressions of an organisation.

Research Summary

Insight 01

Candidates need concise information to quickly understand whether the company is relevant to them.

Insight 02

Candidates want to understand the people, culture, and actual experience behind the company.

Insight 03

Clear and authentic information can reduce uncertainty during the recruitment process.

Target Users

Graduate students who are beginning to research potential employers and are looking for a company that aligns with their values, interests, and career goals.

They need enough context to compare organisations while still being able to quickly find practical recruitment information.

Project Goals

Create a recruitment experience that allows candidates to quickly understand the organisation, discover relevant information, and build confidence in their decision to apply.

The design should balance practical recruitment information with a more human representation of the organisation and its people.

Prototype

Based on the research findings and usability testing, the prototype was refined to improve information hierarchy, navigation, and overall comprehension.

The final design focuses on helping users move naturally from discovery to understanding and finally to application.

The prototype also explores how visual storytelling can communicate the organisation's personality.
