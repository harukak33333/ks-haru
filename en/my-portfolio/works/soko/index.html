#UI/UX Design #User Research #AI Agent

SOKO

AI Partner for Next-Generation Crafts

Summary

SOKO is an AI design partner that helps traditional artisans explore new product ideas for global markets.
Instead of replacing the artisan's creativity, SOKO supports the process from idea generation to refinement and market evaluation.
The project was developed with Nisshin Precious Metals, a heritage company continuing the tradition of Tokyo silverware.

The Problem

Creating a new craft product often requires many rounds of physical prototyping. At the same time, exploring new markets can be difficult without knowing what designs might appeal to different audiences.

The challenge was:
How might we help an artisan explore more ideas with less time and cost, while keeping their creative decisions at the center?

Project goals

  1. Expand creative possibilities
    Help artisans discover product directions inspired by global markets and contemporary design.
  2. Reduce prototyping risk
    Enable artisans to explore and refine ideas digitally before investing in physical prototypes.
  3. Make AI approachable
    Create an interaction model that feels familiar and intuitive, even for first-time AI users.
  4. Connect craftsmanship with market insight
    Help artisans understand how their ideas might be perceived and valued in international markets.
  5. Preserve creative ownership
    Ensure that AI supports the artisan's decisions rather than taking over the creative process.

The opportunity

Instead of moving directly from an idea to a physical prototype, an artisan can explore different possibilities digitally first.

Research Objectives

The research focused on understanding how an artisan develops new ideas and where AI could support that process naturally.

We wanted to learn:

  • How ideas are developed and refined
  • What makes AI tools difficult or uncomfortable to use
  • What kind of feedback is useful during creation
  • How physical craftsmanship could translate into digital interactions
  • How AI could support creativity without taking control

We conducted three interviews with the artisan and design mentors, followed by usability testing and iterative design.

Target Users

A traditional silverware artisan exploring new possibilities. The primary user was an experienced silver craftsman involved in developing new products and exploring international markets.

Soko Kamikawa

He values:

  • Craftsmanship
  • Creative intuition
  • Physical making
  • Quality and authenticity
  • Personal creative control

He also needs to balance creative exploration with the time and cost of physical production. The design therefore focused on making AI feel like a creative partner, not an automated designer.

Research Findings

#1

AI should suggest, not decide

The artisan wanted to explore AI-generated ideas while making the final creative decisions himself.
Design response:
Give the user control to select, reject, adjust, and combine ideas.

#2

Creativity happens through iteration

Craftsmanship is rarely a one-step process. Ideas evolve through repeated adjustments.
Design response:
Allow users to regenerate, compare, and refine ideas without losing previous versions.

#3

Complex AI tools create a learning barrier

The user was not accustomed to AI design tools.
Design response:
We used familiar interaction patterns inspired by chat and media platforms instead of introducing complex AI terminology.

#4

Market data needs to become tangible

Market trends alone are difficult to translate into design decisions.
Design response:
Combine market insights with concrete product ideas and visual examples.

User Flow

The ReCreate stage became the main design focus because it contained the most complex interactions and was closest to the artisan's actual creative process.

Hi-Fi Screens

01. Suggestion — Explore New Possibilities

The Suggestion phase introduces new ideas based on five markets:
USA · Germany · France · China · Taiwan

Each suggestion combines a product image with information about:

  • Market concept
  • Silver usage
  • Japandi elements
  • Product innovation

The goal was to turn unfamiliar market insights into something the artisan could immediately see and explore.

Market Selection

Users first select a target market. The AI then generates product ideas based on that market.

Narrative-Driven Suggestions

Instead of presenting market research as data, each idea is presented as a short story combining the product image and its reasoning.

Saved Ideas

Suggestions are automatically saved so the artisan can revisit them later. Ideas created in ReCreate are also shown alongside their original concepts.

02. ReCreate — Shape the Idea

ReCreate is the core of the experience. The goal was to make AI feel less like a prompt-based tool and more like a creative partner.

The interaction was inspired by the way artisans naturally work:
Choose → Adjust → Experiment → Refine

Narrowing Down the Idea

The AI first generates several variations of the selected concept. The artisan chooses the images and characteristics they like. These choices help the AI create a more focused base concept.

Iterative Regeneration

The artisan can continue regenerating and refining the idea. Previous versions remain visible, making it easy to compare different directions or return to an earlier idea.

Adjust

The Adjust function allows the artisan to make smaller changes without starting over. This mirrors the subtle adjustments made during physical craftsmanship.

Fusion

The Fusion function allows different ideas to be combined. Users can add references through text, saved images, or images from their device.

Ask

The Ask function lets users explore parts of an object that are not visible.

For example:
“Show me the bottom of the cup.”

This helps the artisan consider the idea as a physical object, not just an image.

03. Value — Evaluate the Idea

In the Value phase, the artisan selects a concept and enters an expected price.

AI evaluates the idea across four areas:
Price · Concept · Japandi · Quality

The results are presented through a simple radar chart. Rather than using technical language, the UI provides clear and approachable feedback that can support the artisan's own judgment.

Results

The final experience turned a complex AI workflow into a simple creative journey:
Discover → Create → Refine → Evaluate

The prototype allowed the artisan to:

  • Explore global design directions
  • Compare multiple concepts
  • Refine ideas through repeated iterations
  • Combine different references
  • Explore hidden aspects of an object
  • Consider market value before physical production

Through three rounds of interviews and more than ten design iterations, we refined the experience around the artisan's existing creative process. The result was an AI tool designed to support creative judgment rather than replace it.

AI is not only about what the technology can do

It is also about how people feel when using it. For a craftsperson, creativity is closely connected to experience, intuition, and personal judgment. AI should support those qualities, not take them away. The ReCreate experience especially taught me the importance of control and creative ownership in AI UX. The AI can generate possibilities. The artisan decides what is worth creating.
